## Brand Narrative

Event companies sell an unprovable promise: that a day none of us has seen yet
will go well. Everything on the site is either evidence for that promise or noise
against it.

Positioning for this category is **not** a slogan. It is the answer to: *why
would someone drive past two cheaper organisers to reach this one?*

> **Before running anything:** load `project-eventina-brand` for verified facts.
> Do not invent history, client names, or numbers — a fabricated credential in
> this market is discovered at the first venue conversation.

### Method

1. **Find the refusal.** What work does this company turn down? A company that
   takes everything has no position. If they take everything, find what they are
   *visibly better at* in their own photo archive.
2. **Locate the proof.** Years operating, event count, repeat families, venue
   relationships, the same team on the day. Each claim needs a source or it is
   cut.
3. **Write the spine** — five sentences, below.
4. **Set the voice** with a do/don't table, including register in the local
   language.
5. **Name the competitor set honestly** and state the one axis of difference.

### The story spine

Five sentences. If any needs a sixth to make sense, the positioning is unclear.

```
1. WHO      Families and companies in <region> planning <event types>.
2. TENSION  The day cannot be rehearsed and cannot be repeated.
3. PROMISE  <What this company guarantees that others do not.>
4. PROOF    <Years, count, repeat rate, named venues — sourced.>
5. INVITE   <The single next action.>
```

Sentence 3 is the whole business. "We make your day special" is sentence 3 for
every organiser in India and is therefore not sentence 3 for any of them.

### Tone of voice

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Concrete nouns — *mandap*, *baraat*, *sangeet*, venue names | Category abstractions — "solutions", "experiences", "offerings" |
| Numbers with a source — "310 guests, one venue change, four hours' notice" | Unsourced superlatives — "India's leading" |
| The client's own words in testimonials, lightly trimmed | Rewritten testimonials that all sound like the copywriter |
| Plain verbs — planned, built, fed, lit, moved | Inflated verbs — curated, orchestrated, elevated |
| Credit the team and vendors by name | The passive voice that hides who did the work |

**Register in Marathi and Hindi is not a translation problem.** The formal/
familiar distinction (आपण vs तू) encodes the client relationship. Get it wrong and
the copy reads as either cold or presumptuous. See `multilingual-content`.

### The premium signal — what actually reads as expensive

Ranked by how much they move perceived quality per unit of effort. This ordering
is **this framework's claim**, not a finding from research:

1. **Photographic consistency.** One grade across the whole grid. A single
   over-sharpened, over-saturated frame undoes ten good ones.
2. **Restraint in quantity.** Twelve events shown properly beats sixty in a
   grid. Volume signals a directory; selection signals a point of view.
3. **Specificity in writing.** A named venue and a real problem solved.
4. **Whitespace and slowness.** Density reads as budget.
5. **Silence about price.** Not because price is shameful — because a premium
   buyer expects a conversation, and a number invites comparison shopping.
6. **Everything else** — animation, gradients, custom cursors. Cheap to add,
   which is exactly why they signal little.

Note that items 1–3 are content problems, not design problems. **A portfolio site
cannot out-design its photography.** If the archive is weak, say so early and
budget a shoot; see `photo-curation`.

### Caveats

- **Do not position against the client's actual customers.** "Not for people who
  want cheap" is a positioning statement that insults half the enquiry list.
- **Verify before publishing any claim.** Award, count, or client name — sourced
  or cut. See `project-eventina-brand` for what is currently `[to verify]`.
- **Voice is set by the client, not the agency.** Propose, show two options
  against real photographs, let them choose.

### Checklist

- [ ] The refusal is named — what this company does not do
- [ ] Five-sentence spine written; sentence 3 survives a competitor swap test
- [ ] Every proof point has a source, or is cut
- [ ] Do/don't voice table agreed with the client
- [ ] Local-language register decided, not defaulted
- [ ] Competitor set named and the difference axis stated in one line
- [ ] Photography quality assessed honestly against the positioning
